Why It Matters

A healthy ocean means a healthy planet.

Oceans regulate our climate, provide food and livelihoods, absorb carbon dioxide and support an extraordinary diversity of life. Protecting them is not simply an environmental issue — it is essential for our future.

The Threats

What is harming our oceans?

Human activity has created a growing number of pressures on marine ecosystems.

🧴

Plastic Pollution

Plastic waste can enter marine ecosystems, where it can injure wildlife, degrade habitats and break down into smaller particles.

🌡️

Climate Change

Rising ocean temperatures and increasing acidity place stress on coral reefs, marine organisms and entire ecosystems.

🎣

Overfishing

Unsustainable fishing can reduce populations, disrupt food webs and threaten the livelihoods of communities that depend on the sea.

🛢️

Chemical Pollution

Industrial chemicals, agricultural runoff and other pollutants can enter waterways and damage marine life.

🪸

Habitat Loss

Coastal development, destructive activities and changing conditions can damage mangroves, seagrass beds and coral reefs.

🌊

Coastal Pollution

Waste and untreated pollution entering coastal waters can affect biodiversity, tourism, fisheries and human communities.

The Way Forward

We can change the tide.

Protecting ocean health requires action from individuals, communities, governments, researchers and industries.

01

Reduce Plastic

Reduce unnecessary single-use plastics, improve waste management and support systems that keep plastic out of waterways.

02

Restore Ecosystems

Protect and restore coral reefs, mangroves, seagrass ecosystems and other habitats that support marine life.

03

Support Sustainable Fishing

Strong fisheries management and responsible fishing practices can help marine populations recover while supporting coastal communities.

04

Take Action Together

Education, scientific research, policy and international cooperation can turn awareness into meaningful change.
